# Data integrity audit — `Forex_Data/`

Generated by `run_data_audit.py`. Raw table: [`reports/data_integrity.csv`](../reports/data_integrity.csv).

---

## 1. The headline problem: the 15m files are not 15m

**142 of the 156 `*_15m_real.parquet` files begin with a block of coarser bars** —
daily in 37 files, hourly in 105 — carrying the same schema and the same `_15m_`
filename as the genuine 15-minute data that follows.

Concretely, for `EURUSD_15m_real.parquet`:

| year | rows | median spacing |
|---|---|---|
| 2015–2022 | 223–261 / yr | **86,400 s (1 day)** |
| 2023 | 10,851 | 900 s |
| 2024 | 22,440 | 900 s |
| 2025 | 24,836 | 900 s |
| 2026 | 18,092 | 900 s |

The first 4,863 rows of that file are **byte-identical to `EURUSD_d1_real.parquet`**
(2,096 of the 3,015 overlapping timestamps match exactly on all four OHLC fields).

### Why this matters more than the row count suggests

The padded block is only **3.0 % of rows** but **60 % of the calendar span** the
file advertises. Every indicator computed over the whole file therefore crosses a
resolution boundary silently:

- a 200-period EMA "on 15m" is a 200-**day** EMA for the first eight years;
- `datetime.hour` is `0` for every padded bar, so any hour-of-day or kill-zone
  feature is degenerate there;
- a 3-bar fair-value gap spans 45 minutes after the boundary and **3 days** before it;
- ATR, rolling extremes, RSI and realised volatility all change units mid-series.

### Where genuine 15m data starts

| quarter | instruments |
|---|---|
| 2023 Q2 | 6 |
| 2023 Q3 | 56 |
| 2023 Q4 | 4 |
| 2024 Q1 | 1 |
| **2024 Q3** | **87** |
| 2025 Q1 | 2 |

Usable 15m bars per instrument after clipping: p10 = 50,814, median = 73,043,
p90 = 74,512. **357,601 rows (3.3 % of all 15m rows) are discarded as mis-sampled.**

The practical consequence: *this dataset supports roughly three years of genuine
15-minute research (2023-09 → 2026-09) for about 66 instruments, and two years for
the rest* — not the 2015–2026 span the filenames imply.

The same defect affects the 1h files (median 53 % of span padded) and the 4h files
(55 %). The **D1 files are clean** (median 0.1 % of span) and are the only place
where the advertised 2015+ history is real.

## 3. The `spread` column is the most valuable field in the dataset

Each bar carries the broker's integer spread in **points**; multiplying by the
manifest `point` gives the quoted spread in price units for that bar. This makes a
properly cost-aware backtest possible without assumptions. Median round-turn cost
(spread + slippage + commission) over the pure era:

| instrument | spread (bps) | ATR(14) 15m (bps) | round turn (bps) | cost / ATR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| USDJPY | 0.066 | 6.8 | 0.88 | 0.130 |
| EURUSD | 0.090 | 5.0 | 0.88 | 0.176 |
| XAUUSD | 0.207 | 16.7 | 0.41 | 0.025 |
| NAS100 | 0.519 | 13.4 | 1.04 | 0.078 |
| XAGUSD | 3.406 | — | — | 0.215 |
| EURHUF | 4.055 | — | — | 1.398 |
| LEAD | — | — | — | 2.845 |

**The single most important economic fact for intraday FX on this data:** retail ECN
commission alone (0.70 bp round turn) is ~14 % of one 15-minute ATR on EURUSD. Spread
is almost irrelevant next to it. Any 15m strategy risking ~1 ATR per trade starts
~0.15 R in the hole.

Zero-spread rows (median 5.6 % of 15m rows, concentrated in the padded block) are
handled by falling back to the instrument's own hour-of-day median spread
(`quantlab/costs.py`).

## 4. Impact on `s4_fvg_ml_strategy.py`

S4's first out-of-sample window opens **2023-09-15**. Of its 18 canonical assets:

| | |
|---|---|
| assets with **no genuine 15m data** at that date | **8 / 18** — NICKEL, LEAD, EURCNH, GAS, US2000, NZDCNH, XAUCNH, GAUCNH |
| mean share of each input file that is mis-sampled | 3.8 % of rows / **42 % of calendar span** |
| `GAUCNH` genuine 15m start | **2025-01-23** — i.e. after 17 of S4's 20 "out-of-sample" windows have closed |

Everything S4 learns about those eight instruments before their respective
`pure_start` dates is learned from daily or hourly bars presented as 15-minute bars.

---

## 5. How this repository handles it

`quantlab/datafeed.py` detects the resolution boundary per file with a rolling
spacing test (≥ 80 % of diffs equal to nominal over a 500-bar window) and clips
everything before it. `load_bars(..., enforce_pure=True)` is the default, and the
screening step in `quantlab/universe.py` drops any instrument left with fewer than
20,000 genuine 15m bars.

**Recommendation for the dataset itself:** either rename the padded files to reflect
what they contain, or publish a `pure_start` field per symbol/timeframe in
`manifest.json`. A one-line metadata addition would prevent every downstream user
from rediscovering this.
